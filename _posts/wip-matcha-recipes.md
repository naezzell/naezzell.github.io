# Requisite supplies

At minimum, you will need matcha/hojicha/etc. tea powder, hot water, and something to mix the two together. When I make matcha, I use the following tools

1. An electric, temperature controlled kettle (think Fellow Stagg Pour Over Kettle, but I have a cheaper knock off)
2. A kitchen scale (think Hario V60 Drip Scale, but I have a cheaper knock off)
3. Handheld milk frother (this is what I use to blend the matcha powder with hot water).

Depending on the drink you are making, you will probably also want some milk, ice, and sweetener.

## More details on what I use

In the above list, I didn't specify what type of matcha, milk, and sweetener I use. Here, I compile what I use for reference, but this is by no means the result of exhaustive comparisons, so source what you can and let your taste buds guide where you land! I want to emphasize the source part: I now live in LA where I can find all this stuff nearby, but I used to live in Mississippi, so I am aware of how lucky that is! 

### Matcha
Living in LA, I usually source my matcha from Tea Master of Little Tokyo; specifically, I get the <a href="https://teamastermatcha.com/products/deluxe-culinary-matcha-powder" target="_blank">Deluxe Latte-Grade Culinary Matcha Powder</a>. This choice was guided by trial and error and the reality that I almost exclusively use matcha to make a matcha latte.

On the other hand, if you want to drink matcha straight (no milk or sweetener), higher quality, more expensive matcha is noticeable better than this bag of matcha. Yet, once you add sugar and milk, the subtle differences between really high quality stuff washes out, and I have found it doesn't really get that much better than the above Culinary bag from Tea Master. I found this out by comparing this bag's taste to a range of different priced matcha's I bought in Uji when I visited Japan in the Summer of 2025. 

As a warning, though, it does get worse. As with most hobbies, I first tried the cheapest matcha I could find at local grocery stores (think Nijiya and Mitsuwa), and these were bitter or just plain bad. This is just my opinion, though, and in fact, I would encourage you to try the cheaper ones first. If you already like them, great---stop right there and you will save some money!

### Milk

Honestly, I usually just use Trader Joe's [[https://www.traderjoes.com/home/products/pdp/organic-lactose-free-reduced-fat-milk-082978][Organic Lactose Free Reduced Fat Milk]] as
a compromise between taste, price, my stomach, and family preferences. If I only
cared about taste, though, I would use [[https://horizon.com/organic-dairy-products/organic-milk/organic-whole-milk/][Horizon Organic Whole Milk]].

### Sweetener

I like to mix my sweetener up for fun. I jump between
- Agave (prefered go to)
- Honey (ideally from a farmer's market)
- Maple syrup
- Brown or turbinado sugar
